The idea

The home screen is a 24-hour dial. Every match is an arc at its time of day, the hand is now, and the live ones are the only colour.

Logo

Two rings with one ball between them. It is the dial in miniature: the logo and the product are the same drawing.

Colour

Warm paper and ink, light first. Vermilion means live and nothing else; teams bring their own colours.

Feels like

A Braun clock or a good watch face. The calmest of the three, and the closest to "clean and solid".

Risk

Restraint can read as plain. The dial has to carry the wow.

The idea

A live match sits inside a slowly turning bearing. Upcoming matches each get a small ring that closes as kick-off approaches.

Logo

A lowercase b made of a bar and a ring, in brushed steel, with a hot ball at its centre.

Colour

Graphite and steel, dark first. Orange, like hot metal, means live. Team colours appear as thin edges of light.

Feels like

Your EA FC stage pages: material, depth and motion. The most spectacular of the three.

Risk

Dark steel can tip into "gaming hardware". It needs restraint to stay trustworthy, and a light mode is harder.

The idea

Each live match fills its card with the two teams' colours. Scores and controls sit on glass above, so the brand itself stays almost colourless.

Logo

A single thick ring, open where the red ball sits. It works in one colour and at 16 pixels.

Colour

Mist and ink, light first, with a full dark mode. Apple's red means live. Everything else is borrowed from the teams.

Feels like

A first-party iPhone app. The friendliest of the three, and the most familiar.

Risk

It could look like everyone else's Apple-style app. Team colours that clash will need careful handling.
